Paddle Power Meter Map

A canoe paddle that measures its own power, a phone app that shows it live, and a race simulator that models the stroke, the boat and the course. This is how the pieces fit, drawn from the code as it is on main.

The whole system

There is no server. The "back end" is firmware on two ESP32 boards: one inside the paddle, one on the hull reading the speed impeller. Everything else, the live app and the simulator with its physics engine, is static web pages that run in your browser.

How the paddle gets power

src/PowerModel · Kalman · Orientation

Power is force times speed. The strain gauge gives the force at the blade. The two gyros give how fast the shaft turns. The missing piece is the lever: how far the blade is from the point the shaft turns about (near your top hand), which moves as you paddle. The firmware estimates it live from the difference between the two accelerometers.

The front end

docs/app/

Two pages and two scripts, all static. The app talks to the paddle with Web Bluetooth. The simulator never touches the paddle: it runs the physics engine in the page and keeps each boat's settings in your browser.

The physics engine

stroke-sim.js · fit-sim.js

A quasi-static model for comparing strokes: it steps the paddle through one drive, works out the water's force on the blade at each moment, then finds the boat speed at which your power is used up. Losses come off in a fixed order, and the body and its strength sit to one side, setting how much power you can hold.

Where the power goes, from the engine

The engine's own split of 150 W for the simulator's two starting boats. The rows always add to 100%; efficiency is the first one.

Where the code is

lines, own code only

About 11,700 lines written for this project, plus the vendored sensor libraries in lib/ (BMI270, NAU7802, MAX17048), which are left out here.

FolderWhat's in it
src/Paddle firmware: sensors, filters, the power model, Bluetooth, SD log, boat link. src/boat/ is the boat node.
docs/app/The app, the simulator, and the two engine scripts.
test/host/C++ tests of the firmware maths against a simulated paddle (StrokeSim.h).
test/web/Browser tests in headless Chromium: the app against recorded packets, the simulator's physics and pages.
tools/log2csv.py and replay, which re-runs the firmware maths on an SD log.
lib/Vendored SparkFun sensor libraries.

How it's tested

test/host/run.sh · test/web/run.sh

The firmware's maths is plain C++ with no Arduino code in it, so it builds and runs on a PC against a physics simulation of a paddler. The same C++ writes real Bluetooth packets that the browser test then feeds to the app. The simulator's engine is tested in Node, its pages in Chromium at phone and desktop widths, light and dark.
