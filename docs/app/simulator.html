How this is calculated

The paddle swings about a pivot point on the shaft. Your top hand rises or drops so the blade stays buried to the throat through the drive, as good technique does, so strokes differ by direction and placement rather than by blade depth.

Water pushes on the blade face with drag that grows with the square of the blade's speed through the water. The boat settles where the average forward push equals hull drag (a C1 marathon canoe).

Pushing off the boat's centreline turns it: paddling on the right turns the bow left. The simulator charges for cancelling that turn as a J-stroke behind you, using the blade as a rudder, which adds drag. Both settings are under Steering, so you can see how much the answer depends on them, or set them to match how you correct.

Your power then splits exactly into moving the boat, steering, and slip (churning water). Sideways and vertical pushing aren't charged directly. They make the blade move faster for the same forward push (more slip) and usually turn the boat more (more steering).

This is a simplified model for comparing techniques. It doesn't model boat surge, waves, the blade's lift, or your body. Absolute speeds are approximate; differences between strokes are the useful part.
