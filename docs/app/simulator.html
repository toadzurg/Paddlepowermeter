How this works
  • Blade: pivots on the shaft and stays buried to the throat. Its force grows with the square of its speed through the water. The water hits it at an angle, so part of the force is lift.
  • Boat: a C1 marathon hull, calibrated from a coast-down test. Speed settles where the average forward push equals drag.
  • Steering: off-centre pushes turn the boat. Switching sides fixes it but costs part of a stroke each time, and the zigzag costs distance.
  • Other losses: sideways push makes the hull crab, speed surges through each stroke, and the blade pushes the boat up or down.
  • Race: wind, current, shallow water, and in a river the current across the channel and the inside of bends.

A simplified model for comparing strokes. It treats the flow as steady and ignores chop, wakes, your body moving and your strength in each position. Absolute speeds are rough; the differences are what count.
