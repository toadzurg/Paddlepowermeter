How this is calculated

The paddle swings about a pivot point on the shaft. Your top hand rises or drops so the blade stays buried to the throat through the drive, as good technique does, so strokes differ by direction and placement rather than by blade depth.

Water pushes on the blade face with drag that grows with the square of the blade's speed through the water. The boat settles where the average forward push equals hull drag (a C1 marathon canoe).

Pushing off the boat's centreline turns it: paddling on the right turns the bow left. You correct by switching sides. The hull resists turning in proportion to its speed, so the boat drifts off course at a steady rate, and you switch when it's off by the set heading. Each switch loses part of a stroke. A stroke that turns the boat more means more switches, so more lost time. The hull's turning resistance is set so a clean vertical stroke allows about 8 strokes per side.

Sideways push slows you too. The boat sways under it, and the hull resists by crabbing slightly through the water, like a keel, which creates drag. Brief pushes mostly just sway the boat; steady ones cost the most.

The push comes in pulses, so the boat speeds up in the drive and slows in the recovery. Drag grows with the square of speed, so an uneven speed costs more than a steady one at the same average.

Up and down push: a blade angled forward at the catch pushes water down and lifts the boat; angled back at the exit it lifts water and pulls the boat down. The bent shaft shifts that balance toward the exit. The average changes how deep the boat sits, and hull drag follows wetted area (about weight to the 2/3). The swing bounces the boat on the water, and the waves it makes cost power. Both are small next to slip.

Your power splits exactly into moving the boat, slip (churning water), sideways drag, surge, and bounce and sinking (or a small saving if the stroke lifts the boat).

Wind and current (race): air drag on you and the boat changes with the air speed past you, and current adds to your speed over the ground. Crosswinds and waves aren't modelled.

This is a simplified model for comparing techniques. It doesn't model waves, the blade's lift, or your body moving in the boat. Absolute speeds are approximate; differences between strokes are the useful part.
