    How this works
    • Blade: buried to the throat through the pull. It spears in edge first over the drive's first 15% and is lifting out over its last 15%, moving, so it never stops dead at the catch or the exit, and it's only loaded while fully in. Its force grows with the square of its speed through the water. The water hits it at an angle, so part of the force is lift.
    • Boat: a C1 marathon hull. Drag is from a coast-down test, scaled to the paddler's weight, plus an estimate of wave drag at race pace. Speed settles where the average forward push equals drag.
    • Steering: off-centre pushes turn the boat. Switching sides fixes it but loses the time you set for each switch, and the zigzag costs distance.
    • Other losses: sideways push makes the hull crab, speed surges through each stroke, the blade pushes the boat up or down, and your body's motion (swing, drop, hinge) rocks and bobs the boat.
    • Race: wind (a crosswind turns the bow), current, shallow water, portages, and in a river the current across the channel and the inside of bends. Load the AuSable or your own GPX, with current from USGS gauges and weather from Open-Meteo. Heat and humidity cut the power you can hold.

    A simplified model for comparing strokes. It treats the flow as steady and ignores chop and wakes. The paddler sits with the hips where the blade leaves the water on a plain stroke, paddles every technique from that seat, and the body is fitted to the paddle (lean, twist) so the hands are within reach; it says when a stroke isn't. Seat height sets how stable the boat is (and, with strength by body position, where your body sits against the paddle). Your strength is the power you can hold; with strength by position on (Model assumptions), also an assumed curve of how hard you can pull at each paddle angle or arm position. With gearing on, your arms also hold the most power moving at one speed, so the stroke rate and the stroke's length matter. The Fit tab searches seat height, paddle length and hand placement for the most speed at what you can hold, inside the limits of reach, the top hand's height and stability. Absolute speeds are rough; the differences are what count.
